# My Career Story

## Where it started

I did not grow up planning to be in finance, but I always liked two things: understanding how money moves, and being the person who actually organizes things so they work. In my diploma program in Oman I finished ranked first in my class, which earned me a scholarship for elite students. That result mattered less because of the ranking and more because it taught me that consistent, careful work compounds. That belief is still the core of how I operate.

## Learning by doing

Before I had any title worth putting on a resume, I was getting my hands dirty. In 2023 I took trainee roles at Tibiaan Properties and Msyaaf Group, where I did the unglamorous but essential work — cleaning up thousands of financial records, fixing data integrity, building presentations for events. Around the same time I started Just Brew, a small cold-coffee business. Running it taught me pricing, margins, and the difference between an idea and a business that actually makes money. It is still running.

## Finding finance, then finding data

Arizona State and the W. P. Carey School gave me the structure to turn instinct into skill. I chose a double focus — Finance and Business Data Analytics — on purpose. I did not want to be someone who could only build a model or only read a dashboard. I wanted both.

My internship at OQ was my first real corporate finance experience: consolidated statements, variance analysis, IFRS and GAAP, and the reality of a mid-year close. Then came the experience that confirmed my direction — Equity Capital Markets at Sohar International. Working on live transactions, drafting investor materials, and building valuation models on real GCC companies showed me that this is the work I want to do. Deals are where strategy, numbers, and people meet.

In parallel, my analytics coursework kept proving its value. Building a Python pipeline on retail sales data, and a Microsoft Fabric lakehouse on used-car pricing, taught me that the same rigor I bring to a valuation applies to data: clean inputs, honest assumptions, clear conclusions.

## Leading while learning

I have never wanted to just participate. As Financial Officer of the Omani Student Association, I manage a budget above $30,000, chase down sponsorships, and make sure events for hundreds of people actually happen. As a tutor — and now a lead tutor who evaluates other tutors — I have learned that being good at something and being able to help someone else get good at it are different skills. The second one is harder and more rewarding.

## Where I'm going

I want to build a career in investment banking and corporate finance, the kind of work where I can sit at the deal table and still be the person who can prove the thesis with data. Long term, I see myself moving between global financial centers and the GCC, eventually in a leadership role. The thread through all of it is the same one from my diploma year: careful, consistent work, applied to problems that matter.
